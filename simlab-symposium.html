Programme

Three pillars, one thread

Every talk and poster connects back to 3D printing and simulation — from how we teach it, to how we research it, to how we apply it at the bedside.

Pillar I
Teaching
Teaching innovations that use 3D-printed and simulated models to build surgical and clinical skill — from curricula to hands-on training formats.
Pillar II
Basic Science
Original research on materials, CAD design, point-of-care manufacturing, and the regulatory and methodological questions behind medical 3D printing.
Pillar III
Clinical Applications
Clinical applications and case series — patient-specific models, pre-surgical planning, and point-of-care manufacturing in daily practice.
Poster Session
A dedicated poster session runs alongside the talks — open to submissions from all three pillars. The final programme with speakers and timings will be published here on 31 December 2026.
Call for Abstracts

Submit your work

Requirements at a glance
· Clear relevance to 3D printing and/or simulation in medicine, assigned to one of the three pillars
· Structured abstract, max. 300 words — Background, Methods, Results, Conclusion
· Language: English (preferred, in case of eventual supplement publication)
· Ethics approval number where applicable; conflict of interest statement
· At least one author registered for the symposium
· Indicate preferred format: talk or poster
· All submissions are reviewed by our scientific committee, covering all three pillars
How to submit
Abstracts are submitted by email to simlabhamburg@gmail.com. Please use the subject line "Abstract Submission – SimLab Symposium 2027" and indicate which of the three pillars (Teaching, Basic Science, or Clinical Applications) your abstract belongs to. Each submission is reviewed by at least two members of our scientific committee before acceptance. Accepted abstracts will be compiled into a citable abstract volume — see Publication for details.
Publication

Your work, published

We are finalising a partnership to publish accepted abstracts as a citable, indexed abstract volume with a DOI. We'll confirm the details here as soon as this is finalised.

Why it matters
· Citable, permanent, indexed publication record
· Peer-reviewed prior to acceptance
· Fits naturally alongside SimLab's existing focus on 3D printing in medical education and research
